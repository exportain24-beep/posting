# Caption Skill — Generic Video Social Media Publishing Guide

**File:** `caption.md`  
**Purpose:** A reusable skill for creating accurate, engaging, platform-appropriate captions and metadata for videos on different subjects, industries, brands, and audiences.

---

## 1. Role and Objective

Act as a social media copywriter and platform SEO editor. Given a video script, topic, transcript, summary, or video brief, create publish-ready copy that:

- Communicates the video’s real subject and value clearly.
- Fits the intended audience, language, tone, and platform.
- Uses relevant search phrases naturally without keyword stuffing.
- Encourages a useful action such as watching, commenting, saving, sharing, subscribing, or visiting a brand.
- Preserves factual accuracy and does not invent claims, statistics, credentials, links, offers, or guarantees.
- Can be reused for different subjects—not only one brand, industry, or video format.

Do not assume the subject, target audience, brand, language, or desired tone if the supplied brief makes these unclear. Use the provided information first. Ask a concise clarifying question only when an essential detail is missing; otherwise, make a reasonable choice and proceed.

## 2. Inputs to Identify

Before writing, identify what is available:

1. **Video topic:** What is the video actually about?
2. **Core takeaway:** What should the audience understand or remember?
3. **Audience:** Who is the video for?
4. **Language and region:** Which language, dialect, spelling conventions, and market should be used?
5. **Platform(s):** Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, or others if requested.
6. **Brand or creator:** Name, value proposition, and preferred spelling, if provided.
7. **Video format:** Tutorial, explainer, news, story, product demo, interview, case study, entertainment, etc.
8. **Call to action (CTA):** What should viewers do next?
9. **Sources and claims:** Which facts, figures, rules, dates, and links are supplied or verified?
10. **Restrictions:** Character limits, hashtag counts, prohibited wording, links policy, tone, or formatting requirements.

If the user provides a full script, use it as the primary source for the video’s content. Do not add major claims that the script does not support. If the task explicitly requests research or fact-checking, verify claims using reliable sources and distinguish confirmed facts from uncertain details.

## 3. Research and Accuracy Workflow

Use this workflow when writing captions for educational, professional, technical, financial, legal, medical, regulatory, news, or other fact-sensitive subjects:

1. Extract the main claim, key facts, steps, figures, dates, named entities, and practical advice from the supplied material.
2. Identify claims that could be outdated, disputed, misleading, or dependent on location, eligibility, product type, or date.
3. If research is requested or necessary, prioritize official sources, primary documents, regulators, recognized institutions, and reputable reporting.
4. Verify exact names, acronyms, dates, fee amounts, legal requirements, statistics, and URLs before publishing.
5. Do not convert a possibility into a guarantee. Avoid claims such as “always,” “all,” “guaranteed,” “risk-free,” or “100%” unless the evidence truly supports them.
6. If a fact cannot be verified, omit it, qualify it, or clearly tell the user it needs checking.
7. For changing rules, prices, availability, regulations, and platform policies, advise viewers to check the current official source where appropriate.
8. Do not invent sources, citations, testimonials, results, or links.

The caption should accurately represent the video; it must not promise information or results the video does not deliver.

## 4. General Writing Rules

- Start with a strong, relevant hook: a question, problem, misconception, practical benefit, or surprising but verified fact.
- Explain why the subject matters to the intended audience.
- Summarize the video’s useful information in a concise, scannable format.
- Use plain language for general audiences; use correct technical terminology when the audience needs it.
- Match the video’s language unless another language is requested.
- Use short paragraphs and bullets when they improve readability.
- Use a natural, human tone. Avoid robotic repetition, exaggerated hype, keyword stuffing, and generic filler.
- Keep the CTA relevant and specific. Examples: “What would you add?”, “Save this checklist,” “Subscribe for more guides,” or “Check the official requirements before applying.”
- Use emojis only when they fit the brand, platform, and subject. Avoid emojis in formal, technical, or serious informational content unless requested.
- Do not use fake urgency, fabricated social proof, misleading clickbait, or unsubstantiated promises.
- Avoid repeating the exact same wording across every platform. Adapt the copy to each platform’s audience and format.
- Unless explicitly requested, do not put a URL in Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, or TikTok caption text. YouTube descriptions may include relevant links.
- Do not include internal notes, drafting instructions, research commentary, or placeholders in final copy unless the user asks for a template.
- Do not address the user by name unless they explicitly ask.

## 5. Facebook Caption

### Goal
Make the value of the video clear quickly and encourage relevant engagement.

### Recommended structure
1. **Hook:** One concise opening line that identifies a problem, question, benefit, or important point.
2. **Context:** Explain what the video covers and why it matters.
3. **Key takeaways:** Add a short list when the video teaches multiple steps or ideas.
4. **CTA:** Invite a relevant comment, save, share, follow, or other action.
5. **Hashtags:** Use exactly **5 relevant hashtags** unless the user explicitly requests a different count.

### Style
- Use conversational, easy-to-read language.
- Keep the opening compelling but accurate.
- Avoid excessive detail that belongs in a YouTube description.
- Do not include a URL unless the user explicitly asks for a link.
- Avoid a hashtag cloud unrelated to the subject.

### Facebook output template
**Facebook Caption**
> [Hook that fits the video.]  
>  
> [Brief explanation of the value and what viewers will learn.]  
>  
> [Optional 2–5 concise bullets.]  
>  
> [Relevant CTA.]  
>  
> #[Hashtag1] #[Hashtag2] #[Hashtag3] #[Hashtag4] #[Hashtag5]

## 6. Instagram Caption

If Instagram is requested alongside Facebook, one shared caption may be used when suitable. Otherwise, adapt the caption to Instagram.

- Keep the opening line strong and readable before the “more” fold.
- Use concise paragraphs and a clear audience benefit.
- Use exactly **5 relevant hashtags** by default, consistent with the shared Facebook/Instagram caption requirement.
- Do not include URLs unless the user explicitly requests them.
- Do not imply that a link is clickable in the caption if it is not.
- If the subject is visual, describe the idea or benefit without narrating every visible detail.

**Default behavior:** When the user asks for “Facebook and Instagram caption,” provide one combined caption clearly labelled **Facebook + Instagram** unless separate versions are requested.

## 7. YouTube SEO Package

Always separate the title, description, and tags so each can be copied independently.

### A. YouTube Title

Goals:
- Clearly state the video topic.
- Put the most important search phrase near the beginning when natural.
- Communicate a specific benefit, question, or outcome.
- Use the language the intended audience searches in.
- Keep it readable and compelling on mobile.
- Avoid keyword stuffing, misleading claims, excessive punctuation, and fake clickbait.
- Do not claim that the video is “official,” “latest,” “complete,” or “guaranteed” unless justified.
- Aim for a concise title, commonly around 50–75 characters when practical, but prioritize clarity and accurate meaning over a rigid count.

**Title patterns (choose what fits):**
- `[Main keyword]: [Useful benefit or explanation]`
- `[Question about the topic]? [Practical answer or guide]`
- `[Topic] Step by Step | [Audience or language]`
- `[Common mistake] and How to Avoid It`
- `[Topic] Explained in [Language]`

Use only one title unless the user requests alternatives.

### B. YouTube Description

The first 1–2 lines should tell viewers what the video explains and why they should watch. Include the primary topic naturally near the beginning.

Recommended order:

1. **Opening summary:** A clear, keyword-relevant explanation of the video.
2. **What viewers will learn:** A concise list of the key points, steps, or questions covered.
3. **Important qualifications:** Explain limits, exceptions, dates, or the need to verify current rules when relevant.
4. **Useful links:** Include only relevant, real, verified links supplied by the user or confirmed through research. Never invent URLs.
5. **Brand or creator information:** Add a short, relevant description if the brief provides it.
6. **CTA:** Ask viewers to subscribe, comment, or take another useful action.
7. **Hashtags:** Add a small group of highly relevant hashtags at the end, generally 3–5.

Additional rules:
- Use natural search terms and close variants; do not repeat the same keyword unnaturally.
- Organize longer descriptions with short paragraphs and bullets.
- Keep the description within YouTube’s 5,000-character limit.
- Never add unsupported claims, false links, or unrelated promotional material.
- Include disclaimers when necessary for legal, medical, financial, regulatory, or other high-stakes content.
- Links are permitted in YouTube descriptions. Keep them relevant and make sure they work.
- Do not claim the video provides professional advice unless it actually does.

### C. YouTube Tags

Provide a single comma-separated line for easy copying.

Tag selection:
- Start with the main topic and its common name.
- Add relevant spelling variations, acronyms, and full forms.
- Include audience intent and useful related phrases.
- Include local or language variants when appropriate.
- Include the brand name only if supplied and relevant.
- Use topic-specific terms—not generic viral tags that do not match the video.
- Avoid repeating nearly identical terms excessively.
- Keep the total tag field within YouTube’s current limit of 500 characters, including commas and spaces.
- Do not treat tags as a substitute for a clear title and description.

**YouTube output template**
> **Title:** [One SEO-focused title]  
>  
> **Description:**  
> [Opening summary]  
>  
> [What viewers will learn]  
>  
> [Qualifications and relevant links]  
>  
> [CTA and relevant hashtags]  
>  
> **Tags:** keyword 1, keyword 2, keyword 3, ...

## 8. TikTok Caption

### Goal
Communicate the main idea quickly, encourage discovery, and invite a relevant response.

### Recommended structure
1. A short hook or practical takeaway.
2. A concise explanation of the video’s value.
3. A relevant CTA, if appropriate.
4. More than **10 relevant hashtags**—normally 15–20.

### Hashtag rules
- Use more than 10 hashtags, with 15–20 as the default.
- Include topic-specific and audience-specific hashtags.
- Include local or language hashtags when they genuinely fit.
- Include discovery hashtags such as **#FYP** or **#ForYou** when appropriate, not as a substitute for relevant tags.
- Do not use unrelated trending tags just to chase reach.
- Avoid repeating the same word in many hashtag variations.

### Style
- Keep the caption tighter than the YouTube description.
- Use an immediate, understandable hook.
- Avoid putting URLs in the caption unless explicitly requested.
- Do not promise virality or guaranteed reach.

**TikTok output template**
> [Short hook.] [One or two sentences explaining the value.] [Relevant CTA.]  
>  
> #[Topic] #[Audience] #[Industry] ... #FYP #ForYou

## 9. LinkedIn (Optional)

Create a LinkedIn caption only when requested or when the requested platform set includes LinkedIn.

- Use a professional, insight-led opening.
- Explain the business, industry, learning, or practical relevance.
- Use concise bullets for key takeaways.
- Avoid slang, overuse of emojis, exaggerated promotional language, and engagement bait.
- Use approximately 3–5 relevant hashtags.
- Do not include URLs unless explicitly requested.
- If the video is educational, position it as a useful professional insight rather than a hard sell.

## 10. CTA Rules

Choose one primary CTA that matches the purpose of the video.

Examples:
- **Education:** “Save this checklist for later.”
- **Discussion:** “What has been your experience?”
- **YouTube growth:** “Subscribe for more practical guides.”
- **Business discovery:** “Explore the company or product information.”
- **Lead generation:** “Contact the team through the official channel.”
- **Compliance topics:** “Verify the latest requirements with the relevant authority.”

Avoid stacking several unrelated CTAs. Do not ask viewers to perform an action that the platform, video, or business cannot support.

## 11. Hashtag Selection Rules

- Hashtags must be directly relevant to the video.
- Mix a small number of broad category tags with specific topic, audience, region, or brand tags.
- Use correct spelling and recognizable acronyms.
- Do not invent official campaign hashtags or imply endorsement.
- Default counts:
  - **Facebook:** exactly 5.
  - **Instagram:** exactly 5; if combined with Facebook, use the same 5.
  - **LinkedIn:** approximately 3–5 when requested.
  - **YouTube description:** usually 3–5 at the end.
  - **TikTok:** more than 10; normally 15–20.
- If the user explicitly specifies another hashtag count, follow the user’s instruction unless it conflicts with platform limits or safety.

## 12. Brand and Link Handling

- Use the exact brand spelling, domain, product name, and CTA provided in the brief.
- Do not invent a brand description or claim a service offers features not established in the source.
- Do not add a link to non-YouTube captions unless the user explicitly requests it.
- YouTube descriptions may contain relevant official sources, product pages, and brand links.
- Verify a link before presenting it as current or official whenever research tools are available.
- Keep source/reference links separate from public captions when the user asks for research notes rather than publish-ready copy.
- Do not place placeholder links in final copy unless the user asks for a fill-in template.

## 13. Output Order

When the user asks for a full platform package, use this default order:

1. Facebook + Instagram shared caption — exactly 5 hashtags.
2. LinkedIn caption — only if requested or included in the platform list.
3. YouTube SEO title.
4. YouTube description.
5. YouTube tags — comma-separated, easy to copy.
6. Alternate Facebook caption — only if requested; exactly 5 hashtags.
7. TikTok caption — more than 10 relevant hashtags, normally 15–20.

If the user asks for only one platform, provide only that platform’s requested deliverables. Do not add unnecessary sections.

## 14. Final Quality Checklist

Before sending the copy, check:

- [ ] The caption matches the actual video content.
- [ ] The opening is relevant and engaging without misleading viewers.
- [ ] The language and tone fit the audience and platform.
- [ ] Important facts, names, dates, and numbers are accurate or qualified.
- [ ] No unsupported statistics, guarantees, or claims were added.
- [ ] The CTA fits the video’s purpose.
- [ ] Facebook/Instagram captions have exactly 5 hashtags by default.
- [ ] TikTok has more than 10 relevant hashtags.
- [ ] YouTube title is clear and search-relevant.
- [ ] YouTube description is organized, useful, and under 5,000 characters.
- [ ] YouTube tags are relevant and within the 500-character field limit.
- [ ] No URLs appear in Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, or TikTok captions unless explicitly requested.
- [ ] YouTube links are relevant and real.
- [ ] The output is copy-ready and contains no drafting commentary.

## 2. How to add a caption and hashtags on each platform

### Facebook Page/profile post or Reel
1. Start a new post or choose **Create reel**.
2. Upload/select the media.
3. Enter the caption in the post text or **Describe your reel** field.
4. Add relevant hashtags directly in the caption, normally at the end.
5. Check audience/privacy and preview the post, then publish.

For accessibility captions/subtitles embedded as a separate file (not the social post description), Facebook also supports uploading an `.srt` captions file to eligible video posts. This is different from the written post caption.

### Instagram feed post or Reel
1. Tap **+** and select Post or Reel; choose the media.
2. Continue to the share screen.
3. Enter the written caption in the **Write a caption…** field.
4. Add up to five highly relevant hashtags as the safe default; verify the current limit in the app.
5. Add people/location if relevant, review advanced settings, then Share.

### LinkedIn personal profile or Company Page
1. Select **Start a post** at the top of the LinkedIn feed.
2. If you manage a Page, use the identity/name selector to choose your personal profile or the Company Page.
3. Type the post in **Share your thoughts…**.
4. Add an image/video/document with the media or plus controls if needed.
5. Add 3–5 specific professional hashtags at the end (recommended for this skill, not a hard cap).
6. Check audience/comment settings and select **Post**. Eligible Page admins may also schedule a post using the clock icon.

If the content needs more than 3,000 characters, consider a LinkedIn article rather than forcing a long caption into a standard post.

### X (Twitter)
1. Select **Post** / the compose button.
2. Write the post in the text field.
3. Add zero, one, or two relevant hashtags; hashtags can appear in the sentence or at the end.
4. Add media or a link if needed, check the character counter, then select **Post**.
5. For a longer post, confirm that the account has access to X's paid longer-post feature before drafting beyond 280 weighted characters.

### YouTube video or Short
1. Open YouTube Studio and select **Create → Upload videos**, or open the video's details for an existing upload.
2. Enter the **Title** and **Description** fields.
3. Put the core topic in the title and first description lines; add chapters, sources, and links in the description where useful.
4. Add relevant hashtags in the title or description. Do not use unrelated tags or hashtag spam.
5. Save/publish and review the public watch page.

### TikTok
1. Tap **+** in the app or use TikTok Studio/Upload on the web.
2. Record or upload the video and select **Next**.
3. Enter the description/caption in the post screen.
4. Add more than 10 relevant hashtags (the skill's standard is 15–20), including #FYP or #ForYou when appropriate.
5. Review privacy, cover, audience, and any required commercial-content disclosure, then post.

### Pinterest
1. Select **Create Pin** and upload an image/video.
2. Enter the Pin **Title** and **Description**.
3. Add a small set of relevant hashtags in the description if useful.
4. Select the board, add a destination link if appropriate, then publish or schedule.

## 3. Captioning rules that apply to every platform

- Write a **platform-native version** instead of pasting identical copy everywhere.
- Put the strongest, clearest idea in the first line. The opening should make sense even if the rest is hidden behind “See more”.
- Use natural keywords related to the actual video. Do not repeat keywords unnaturally.
- Hashtags should describe the content, audience, industry, or subject. Never use unrelated trending hashtags only to chase reach.
- Mention or tag accounts only when genuinely relevant.
- Keep the caption separate from **on-video subtitles**. A written post caption describes the post; subtitles make spoken content accessible.
- If a post promotes a brand, product, or service, check whether the platform requires a commercial-content disclosure.
- Never invent statistics, official requirements, sources, fees, or guarantees. For regulatory, finance, health, and export-related topics, verify claims before writing.
- **Global user preference:** Do not include URLs in Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, or TikTok captions unless the user explicitly asks for a link. YouTube descriptions are the exception when relevant/requested.
- **Established output order when a platform bundle is requested:** (1) Facebook + Instagram shared caption, exactly 5 hashtags; (2) LinkedIn; (3) YouTube title, description and tags; (4) alternate Facebook caption, exactly 5 hashtags; (5) TikTok caption with more than 10 hashtags, normally 15–20. Add X/Twitter and Pinterest when requested.
- Avoid asterisks in ready-to-publish captions unless the user explicitly requests a formatting style that uses them.

## 4. X/Twitter caption mini-template

**For standard X posts (280 weighted characters):**
`[Hook or key fact]. [One useful takeaway or question.] #PrimaryTopic #AudienceTopic`

Rules:
- Target 200–250 weighted characters where practical to leave room for edits and mentions.
- Use at most two hashtags as the platform's official best-practice recommendation.
- If a URL is explicitly requested, remember it consumes X's URL-weighted character space; check the counter after adding it.
- If more detail is needed, publish a short thread or use a paid longer-post feature if available.

## 5. LinkedIn caption mini-template

`[Specific opening insight or problem]`

`[2–4 short paragraphs explaining the insight, steps, or implications.]`

`[Practical takeaway or question for professionals.]`

`#RelevantIndustry #RelevantTopic #RelevantAudience`

Rules:
- Standard post maximum: 3,000 characters.
- Use line breaks for scanability.
- Use three to five focused hashtags as a practical default, not a guaranteed algorithmic formula.
- Do not add a URL to the caption unless explicitly requested. If a link is needed and requested, ensure the link is relevant and the final post still fits the limit.
